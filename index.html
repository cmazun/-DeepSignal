THE SCIENCE OF DISCOVERY

Find connections. Test explanations.

DeepSignal screens data for relationships worth investigating—not proof of cause and effect. Learn what each statistic means before trusting a discovery.

01 · CORRELATION

What is Pearson's r?

r measures the direction and strength of a linear relationship, from −1 to +1.

−10+1

−1: perfect negative · 0: no linear relationship · +1: perfect positive. An r of 0.9 is strong, but not necessarily meaningful or causal. Curved relationships can have r near zero.

02 · CAUSATION

Does one thing cause another?

Correlation alone cannot establish causation. Causal evidence requires a credible study design, such as randomized experiments or carefully justified natural experiments, and evaluation of confounding, reverse causation, selection bias, and timing.

DeepSignal currently detects correlations, not causes. Future causal-analysis tools will assess evidence, not automatically certify breakthroughs.

03 · VALIDATION

How to trust a discovery

Check sample size, measurement quality, missing values, outliers, multiple comparisons, independent replication, and whether a relationship persists after controlling for other variables.

For health research, population-level associations must not be mistaken for individual-level effects.

Understanding r, p-values, confounders and false discoveries

r: Pearson's correlation coefficient. Its sign indicates direction; magnitude indicates linear strength. r²: fraction of variance explained by a simple linear regression with an intercept—not the probability of causation.

p-value: How surprising a test statistic would be under a specified null hypothesis, not the probability the hypothesis is true. Multiple testing: Screening thousands of pairs produces chance associations; false-discovery-rate adjustment and out-of-sample validation are essential.

Confounder: A third variable that influences both measured variables. Reverse causation: The apparent outcome may influence the supposed cause. Ecological fallacy: A country-level correlation need not hold for individuals.
